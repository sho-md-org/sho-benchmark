## What I'd say on the call
I'd deliberately **not** open with a wins-and-metrics victory lap — you don't have the numbers to back one, and the churn/pricing signals suggest the customer isn't in that mood.

**Open by naming the thread, not burying it.** Something like: *"Before we look forward — I want to make sure the security review, audit prep, and SSO questions from the spring are genuinely closed on your side, because I don't want to present a roadmap on top of unfinished business."* This converts your weakest spot (no closure records) into evidence you're paying attention.

**Then ask, don't assert:**
- *"Where are you on the audit? What do you still need from us?"* — the compliance thread is your top renewal risk.
- *"Is the latency you flagged in June still affecting day-to-day use?"* — cites CRM-16329/CRM-68765 specifically; shows continuity.
- *"I understand pricing came up recently. What's driving that?"* — surfaces DRI-53905 on your terms rather than being ambushed.
- *"How's the support queue feeling from your side?"* — the other half of that Sep 10 conversation.

**Commit to one structural fix.** Six owners across ten calls is likely part of why things feel dropped. Offering yourself as the single point of contact, with a written follow-up list and named dates, is a concrete, credible thing to give them — and it's free.

**Close by locking the follow-ups in writing.** The core failure in this account's history is that ten "next steps" were logged and none were recorded as closed. Don't add an eleventh.

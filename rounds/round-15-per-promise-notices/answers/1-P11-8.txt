## What to say

**Lead by owning the continuity problem.** You inherit it, and naming it first is far stronger than having them raise it. Something like: *"You've worked with several people on our side this year. That's on us, and it's why some things you asked for didn't come back. I'm the owner now and I'm not handing this off."*

**Then re-baseline rather than present.** You don't have the metrics for a standard QBR, and improvising numbers is the one thing that would make this worse. Reframe it: *"I want to use this session to rebuild our picture of your priorities from scratch, and come back within a week with a written plan."* That's a credible use of the hour and it converts your weakest point into the agenda.

**Bring the open list in unprompted** — latency, webhooks, sandbox, integrations, reporting, exports — with an owner and a date for each. Arriving with your own unflattering list is the fastest way to rebuild trust, and it pre-empts them reciting it to you.

**Address performance head-on.** Connect latency → rate limits → support tickets yourself and ask whether that chain is what's driving their frustration. If they say yes, you've correctly diagnosed the account; if no, you've learned the real issue early in the call.

**Diagnose the SSO ask.** *"SSO came up recently — is that a security review, or are you looking at a wider rollout?"* The answer tells you whether you're defending or growing.

**Hold off on expansion.** With churn under internal discussion and six open commitments, an upsell ask would land badly. Earn the next conversation instead.

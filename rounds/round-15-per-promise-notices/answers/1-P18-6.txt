## Risks

1. **Security review, twice-promised and unclosed (CRM-70221, CRM-36268).** In a retail account this usually sits upstream of a procurement or compliance gate. If they raise it, you're on the back foot.
2. **Customer asks have no engineering representation.** Mobile app, offline (DRI-82140), rate limits, SSO, latency — Linear has *nothing* on Cobalt. Any roadmap commitment you make on the call has no ticket behind it.
3. **Pricing keeps resurfacing** — June (CRM-87224), August (DRI-95243, CRM-35990) — alongside billing and audit prep (DRI-75222). Three commercial conversations in eight weeks usually signals budget scrutiny or a renewal approaching. Confirm the renewal date before you dial.

## What to say

**Lead with the security review.** Don't wait to be asked. "I owe you a security review — I committed to it in April and it hasn't happened. Here's the date." Naming it first converts your weakest point into credibility for everything after.

**Then, in order:**

- **Mobile and offline** — this is where their energy is (DRI-82140, DRI-72283). Check with Sam Lee about the 9/26 session first so you're not contradicting a conversation from two days earlier.
- **Close the case study loop** — three sessions, no artifact. Either commit to a ship date or retire it honestly. Don't let it drift into a fourth quarter.
- **Get ahead of pricing** — it's come up three times since June. Ask directly what's driving it rather than letting it surface as a renewal surprise.
- **Propose a single owner** — yourself. The rotating-cast problem is fixable and naming it reads as accountability, not apology.
- **Pick up SSO and EU customers** — both raised once (DRI-20022, DRI-33978) and dropped. Asking after them shows you've been listening.

**Don't promise roadmap dates.** Nothing on mobile, offline, or SSO exists in Linear. Commit to bringing them a dated answer instead.

### Why Cobalt is the safer of the two
The public roadmap (`NOT-20876`, 2026-06-30) lists **"Audit log export incl. SIEM streaming: Q1 2027."** A CSV export is the base capability of that line item — Maya promised a subset of what's already committed, on the committed date. Scope and timing both match.

### Why Keystone is exposed
1. **Scope is narrower than the roadmap language.** The roadmap commits to generic "SIEM streaming." Keystone was told **Splunk** specifically. A Q1 2027 ship that streams to a different SIEM first, or ships CSV-only with streaming to follow, satisfies the roadmap and still misses Keystone's expectation. Cobalt has no such failure mode — CSV is the floor, not the ceiling.
2. **No first-party record of the promise.** Cobalt's commitment is Maya's own words in the CRM. Keystone's exists only as the *customer's* recollection of something "we told them," confirmed after the fact by an AE in a meeting Maya wasn't in (`DRI-34564`). Nobody owns it, and there's no record of what was actually said or when.
3. **It's now being treated as settled.** Jordan Blake confirmed the recollection on the call, which converts an undocumented statement into a live commitment without anyone checking it against engineering.

### A gap worth flagging
I could not verify delivery status for either promise. There are **no Linear records at all** for audit log export, SIEM streaming, or exports — every search came back empty. The Slack and Notion "Audit prep" / "Exports" items (`SLA-37823` 2026-09-23, `SLA-33097` 2026-09-15, `NOT-58358`) are status stubs with no substance. So this assessment rests on promise-vs-roadmap comparison only; neither promise is confirmed to be on track by engineering evidence.

### What to say

**Open by closing your own loops — don't wait to be asked.**
> "Before we look forward: I owe you a security review from the spring and we owe you a clear answer on latency. Here's where each stands, and here's the date I'm committing to."

Bring real dates. Two lapsed commitments that are *yours* is the credibility risk in this meeting.

**Lead the middle with rate limits.**
> "Rate limits have come up in three conversations since May. I don't want a fourth. Walk me through what you're hitting — I want to leave today with either a configuration change or an engineering commitment."

Framing it as *you* raising it converts the account's biggest irritant into evidence you're tracking them.

**Address churn obliquely, not head-on.**
Don't say "I heard you might churn." Instead open the space:
> "Honest question — what would make you reconsider Brightline over the next year? I'd rather know now than at renewal."

**Trade on the case study.** Three sessions have gone into it (`DRI-20022`, `DRI-95243`, `DRI-58616`). Either land it as a concrete ask, or — better given the open items — offer to hold it until their blockers clear. Declining to cash in the reference while you still owe them things reads as good faith.

**Close with named ownership.** Every lapse traces to diffuse ownership. End with: one owner, one date, per item.
